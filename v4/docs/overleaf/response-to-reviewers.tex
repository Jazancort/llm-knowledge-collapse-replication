\section{Reviewer 1}

\subsection*{R1.1 --- Prospective calibration of effective training pressure}

\begin{rcomment}
How can effective training pressure be quantitatively calibrated and
prospectively tested on an unseen backbone or dataset?
\end{rcomment}

The present experiments do not establish a transferable scalar ETP predictor
or a prospective threshold for an unseen backbone or dataset. The revised
manuscript distinguishes controllable design variables (nominal adapter rank,
learning rate, planned training steps, and synthetic-data dose) from
post-training diagnostics, including effective rank and weight drift. Under
the tested protocol, we identify empirical intervals between adjacent
configurations exhibiting different retention trajectories; these intervals
should not be interpreted as universal numerical thresholds.

A prospective test would require specifying a calibration rule and outcome
criterion before examining a held-out backbone or dataset, selecting
configurations on both sides of the predicted regime boundary, and
evaluating them without refitting that rule to the held-out outcomes. We
have added this as a concrete validation protocol in Section~6 and state
explicitly that such held-out prospective validation has not been performed
in the present study. Accordingly, we describe effective training pressure
as an \emph{operational experimental framework}, not a validated universal
predictor.

The pre-registered G5 experiment (three single-seed cells on Qwen) provides
limited exploratory evidence: retention was ordered in the expected direction
for 2 of 3 pre-specified configurations (the degradative cell was classified
as bounded rather than degradative). No operational $\Pi$ threshold was
reported for G5 because the threshold was not pre-specified, and the
experiment does not test an unseen backbone or dataset. Prospective
calibration remains future work.

\msloc{Section~3.5 (ETP as framework, revised); Section~6
(prospective calibration protocol added); Section~4.4 (G5 prospective results).}

\subsection*{R1.2 --- Separating confounds from pressure effects}

\begin{rcomment}
How are quantization, optimizer state, data order, and model-specific
dynamics separated from the claimed pressure effects?
\end{rcomment}

The revised manuscript addresses each potential confound through a dedicated
control, now documented in Section~3.9 with Table~2 (tab:controls).

Regarding quantization: all QLoRA experiments use 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4)
quantization with adapters trained in bf16. Ablation G3 reruns the same
protocol with standard bf16 precision and no base-weight quantization,
three seeds each at $r=16$ and $r=256$. At $r=16$, bf16 achieves
Gen10\,=\,97.4\% (76/78 per seed), while the original NF4 single-seed
result was 96.2\% (75/78); these use different seeds and pipelines and
are not a paired comparison. At $r=256$, bf16 achieves Gen10\,=\,79.1\%,
within 0.0\,pp of the NF4 G1$+$G4 mean under the same seeds and matched
protocol. Quantization is therefore not the primary driver of the observed
regime separation.

Regarding optimizer state: a fresh optimizer is instantiated at every
generation; no momentum or adaptive-rate state is carried forward between
generations. This is held constant across all conditions within a backbone.

Regarding data order: ablation G4 runs Qwen at $r=256$ with three
independent shuffle seeds (42/101/202), keeping all other conditions
identical to G1 (seeds~15/137/256). A permutation test comparing Gen10
retention between the two seed groups gives $p=0.70$ (two-sided, $N=3+3$).
Because seed and data order varied simultaneously in G4, this result does
not isolate the effect of order alone; it establishes that the combined
variance is statistically indistinguishable from the seed-to-seed variance
already present within G1. The combined $N=6$ seed set (G1$+$G4) yields
Gen10\,=\,$79.7\%\pm1.7$\,pp.

Regarding ETP-design versus ETP-observed: we now separate two classes of
ETP variables. \emph{ETP-design} variables are known before training (nominal
rank, learning rate, training steps, and synthetic exposure fraction) and are
directly manipulated. \emph{ETP-observed} variables are measured after
training (effective rank, mean absolute weight drift $d(t)$, response length,
and SDI-3) and serve as post-hoc diagnostic proxies, not pre-specified
predictors.

For the central Qwen result, the $r=16$ condition is represented by three
G3/bf16 runs (seeds~15/137/256, Gen10\,=\,76/78 per seed) and $r=256$ by
six G1$+$G4 runs (Gen10\,=\,61, 63, 61, 64, 61, 63 out of 78; mean
$79.7\%\pm1.7$\,pp). Seeds 15, 137, and 256 appear in both groups, which
differ simultaneously in rank, quantization, and data-order protocol; the
17.7\,pp arithmetic gap is therefore reported as a descriptive observation,
not a confirmatory permutation test. The cleanest rank-only comparison is
the G3 within-protocol design (same seeds, same bf16, rank the only
variable): per-seed Gen10 differences are $+15$, $+13$, and $+15$ items
out of 78, mean $+18.4$\,pp; sign-flip $p=0.25$ ($N=3$, all positive
direction but underpowered).

For Gemma~3, the $r=4$ versus $r=16$ Gen10 contrast ($N=5+5$) gives
$\Delta=+37.8$\,pp (from raw counts, $|K_0|=46$). If the same five seeds
were used at both ranks (paired design), the exact sign-flip test gives
$p=0.0625$ two-sided; if seeds are independent, a bilateral permutation test
over $\binom{10}{5}=252$ allocations gives $p=0.0079$. The seed assignment
across ranks is not confirmed in the archived data; we report both tests.

\msloc{Section~3.5 (ETP definition); Table~1 (tab:notation); Section~3.9
(tab:controls); Section~4.2 (Qwen, G3 paired contrast, 17.7\,pp descriptive
gap); Section~4.3.1 (Gemma~3, $p=0.0625$ sign-flip or $p=0.0079$
unpaired permutation); Section~4.6 (G3 and G4 controls).}

\subsection*{R1.3 --- External validity of the evaluation}

\begin{rcomment}
Do the conclusions hold for longer answers, multi-hop questions, other
domains, and larger factual evaluation sets?
\end{rcomment}

Three distinct concerns are raised: the evaluation metric, the dataset scope,
and the extension to other domains.

On verbosity bias: the primary metric uses a bidirectional substring
criterion, intentionally permissive to handle short-answer variation and
aliasing. Configurations that generate longer responses may receive inflated
retention scores. To address this, we performed a strict exact-match
re-evaluation on the saved predictions for all reported conditions. Under
the strict scorer, absolute retention values are lower, but the direction
of every regime classification is unchanged. This re-evaluation is reported
in supplementary Table~S2 and discussed in Section~6.

On dataset scope: TriviaQA was chosen to isolate factual retention from
reasoning or stylistic confounds. Short answers minimise format-dependent
evaluation noise, and the substring criterion is resilient to verbosity
changes. The limitation is that findings may not transfer to long-form
generation, multi-hop reasoning, open-ended dialogue, or code synthesis;
this scope restriction is stated explicitly in Section~6.

On evaluation set size: the held-out set contains 200 questions, of which
$K_0$ is the set answered correctly at generation~0 (e.g., $|K_0|=78$ for
Qwen). This set is disjoint from the 2,000-question synthetic training stream.
Scaling the evaluation set would alter the training-data composition and is
deferred to future work.

\msloc{Section~6, ll.~2094--2100; supplementary Table~S2;
Section~3.1 (evaluation protocol).}

\subsection*{R1.4 --- Exposure reduction: correction, pre-registered result, and mechanism}

\begin{rcomment}
Why does the five-percent exposure reduction stabilize Qwen but not
Gemma~3, and does this generalize across ranks and seeds?
\end{rcomment}

This question led directly to identifying a pipeline inconsistency in the
original submission and to a substantially revised, pre-registered experiment.

The original submission reported that a ${\sim}5\%$ reduction of synthetic
training examples raised retention by $+9.0$ and $+9.4$\,pp for conditions
C3 and C5 respectively. We subsequently identified that C1 (the baseline)
and C5 (the intervention) had been produced by different evaluation
pipelines, differing in the $K_0$ construction procedure and the data-loading
code path. When C1 and C5 are re-run under an identical pipeline with paired
seeds, the effect of ${\sim}5\%$ example removal is only $+1.9$\,pp (not
distinguishable from zero at $n=3$). This discrepancy is acknowledged
explicitly in the revised manuscript, and the original C2--C5 results are
archived in the replication repository for transparency. This error was
uncovered as a direct result of the reviewer's question.

To replace the discredited result, we designed and pre-registered a
dose--response experiment (protocol committed as \texttt{a916ebf} before any
run), varying synthetic exposure at 0\%, 10\%, 25\%, and 50\% removal across
five paired seeds on Qwen~2.5~1.5B at $r=256$. At Gen~5, the gains are
$+3.6$\,pp (10\%, $p=0.009$), $+3.8$\,pp (25\%, $p=0.003$), and
$+4.4$\,pp (50\%, $p<0.001$), $n=5$ seeds each. The Gen~5 benefit is
uniform across doses while the Gen~10 benefit is dose-graded: $+1.8$\,pp
at 10\% ($p=0.160$, not significant), $+4.9$\,pp at 25\% (95\,\% CI
$[2.5, 7.2]$, $p=0.005$), and $+9.2$\,pp at 50\% (95\,\% CI
$[6.9, 11.6]$, $p<0.001$). At 50\% removal the Gen~5$\to$10 slope is
$-0.15$\,pp/generation versus $-1.13$\,pp/generation at 0\% (mean
per-seed difference of Gen10$-$Gen5 counts divided by five generations),
strongly attenuating progressive loss and shifting the system toward the
Bounded regime.

The pre-registered prediction that 50\% removal would match halving the
learning rate from $\eta=10^{-5}$ to $5\times10^{-6}$ yielded a similar
point estimate: Gen~5 retention at dose\,=\,50\% is 89.7\% versus 90.2\%
at $\eta=5\times10^{-6}$ (mean over three seeds; Gen~10 difference
$+0.4$\,pp, 90\,\% CI $[-0.8, 1.6]$). Formal equivalence was not
established.

A parallel pre-registered dose--response experiment on Gemma~3~1B~IT at
$r=10$ (G2, three paired seeds, $|K_{0,\mathrm{G2}}|=44$) shows that a
50\% reduction raises Gen~10 retention by $\GtwoDeltatenFifty$\,pp
(95\,\% CI $[\GtwoCItenLoFifty, \GtwoCItenHiFifty]$, $p=\GtwoPtenFifty$).
However, the Gen~5$\to$10 slope in the G2 50\% arm ($-0.91$\,pp/generation)
equals that of the 0\% arm ($-0.91$\,pp/generation) within rounding,
indicating that the exposure effect on Gemma~3 manifested as a Gen~10
retention gain rather than as a reduction in the rate of progressive loss.
The mechanism was not established; a steps-matched control was inconclusive
at $n=3$.

\msloc{Section~4.5 (B7 Qwen and G2 Gemma~3 dose--response; pre-registration;
LR equivalence); Section~6 (mechanism limitation); replication repository
(original C1--C5 results archived).}

\subsection*{R2.2 --- Equations and tables}

\begin{rcomment}
Equations 1--6 are malformed in the PDF. Eq.~2 for effective rank is missing
the negative sign and summation. Eq.~6 for SDI-3 has unclear signs and terms.
``$5\times10^6$'' in Table~7 appears to be a rendering error.
\end{rcomment}

The malformed rendering was a font-encoding artefact: Unicode characters in
mathematical mode were not mapped correctly to the T1 font metric. The revised
submission uses the \texttt{lmodern} package with T1 encoding throughout,
which eliminates the rendering artefact.

Eq.~1 now reads:
\[
  R(t) = \frac{|\{q \in K_0 : q \text{ correct at gen.\ } t\}|}{|K_0|}.
\]

Eq.~2 now reads:
\[
  \mathrm{erank}(BA) =
  \exp\!\left(-\sum_i \hat{\sigma}_i \log \hat{\sigma}_i\right),
\]
where $\hat{\sigma}_i = \sigma_i / \sum_j \sigma_j$ are the normalised
singular values of $BA$, distinct from the raw singular values $\sigma_i$,
consistent with Table~1. This is the standard effective-rank definition of
\citet{roy2007}.

Eq.~6 now reads:
\[
  \mathrm{SDI\text{-}3} =
  \log\frac{\mathrm{MeanLen}_T}{\mathrm{MeanLen}_0}
  + \log\frac{\mathrm{D1}_0}{\mathrm{D1}_T}
  + \Delta I.
\]
The first term is positive when mean response length grows; the second is
positive when Distinct-1 diversity declines; and $\Delta I$ captures
item-level instability accumulation. A positive SDI-3 score indicates
cumulative distributional deterioration.

The value in Table~7 is the learning rate $5\times10^{-6}$; the exponent
$-6$ was dropped in the original PDF by the same encoding issue. The revised
PDF renders the value correctly throughout.

Eq.~6, ll.~881--886; Table~7 (tab:ranklr), l.~1473.}

\subsection*{R2.4 --- Methodology}

\begin{rcomment}
No formal statistical tests or confidence intervals. ETP framework is not
quantified. Cross-architecture normalisation failed with no alternative.
ETP is a conceptual label, not a new quantitative measure. Gemma~4 E2B
lacks a citation. ``Sharp transition'' claim weakened by sparse rank grids.
\end{rcomment}

The revised manuscript adds seed-level exact permutation tests for the
comparisons where independent training runs are available, treating seeds and
runs as the experimental unit. For Qwen, the 17.7\,pp arithmetic gap between
$r=16$ and $r=256$ is reported as a descriptive observation because the two
groups are not exchangeable under a rank-only null (they differ simultaneously
in rank, quantization, and data-order protocol). The cleanest within-protocol

unmodified baseline.

ETP is presented as an \emph{operational experimental framework} rather than a
validated scalar predictor. We distinguish ETP-design variables (rank,
learning rate, exposure fraction, training steps) from ETP-observed
post-training diagnostics (effective rank, $d(t)$, response length, SDI-3).
The product $\mathrm{erank}\times\mathrm{LR}$ is an exploratory index labelled
as such; it is not used as a prospective predictor.

The failed normalisations in Table~6 do not provide a universal scalar;
the table is retained as evidence of incommensurability. Architecture-specific
calibration requires a held-out backbone or a pre-specified normalization
rule with out-of-sample validation, deferred to future work.

The contribution is the factorial design, the dose--response evidence across
three backbones, the pre-registered exposure result, and the identification of
architecture-specific regime boundaries, not the ETP label itself.

The Gemma~4 E2B backbone is now cited as \citet{gemma4_2026} (Gemma Team,
2026, arXiv:2607.02770) at every introduction point. Architecture details are
sourced from the released model configuration in the same technical report.

``Sharp transition'' has been replaced throughout with \textcolor{chgcolor}{``abrupt,
threshold-like transition within the tested grid''}, reflecting that the
boundary is established empirically between adjacent rank values rather than
identified at a precise point.

\rconcern{Reviewer \#1}{Concern \#1}{Prospective calibration of ETP}

\rquote{How can effective training pressure be quantitatively calibrated and
prospectively tested on an unseen backbone or dataset?}

\arhead{Author response:}
The present experiments do not establish a transferable scalar ETP predictor
or a prospective threshold for an unseen backbone or dataset. The revised
manuscript distinguishes controllable design variables (nominal adapter rank,
learning rate, planned training steps, and synthetic-data dose) from
post-training diagnostics, including effective rank and weight drift. Under
the tested protocol, we identify empirical intervals between adjacent
configurations exhibiting different retention trajectories; these intervals
should not be interpreted as universal numerical thresholds.

A prospective test would require specifying a calibration rule and outcome
criterion before examining a held-out backbone or dataset, selecting
configurations on both sides of the predicted regime boundary, and evaluating
them without refitting that rule to the held-out outcomes. We describe
effective training pressure as an \emph{operational experimental framework},
not a validated universal predictor. The pre-registered G5 experiment (three
single-seed cells on Qwen) provides limited exploratory evidence: retention
was ordered in the expected direction for 2 of 3 pre-specified configurations.
No $\Pi$ threshold was reported for G5 because the threshold was not
pre-specified, and the experiment does not test an unseen backbone or dataset.
Prospective calibration remains future work.

\arhead{Author action:}
We added a concrete validation protocol for future prospective calibration
in Section~6 and state explicitly that held-out prospective validation has
not been performed in the present study. Section~3.5 was revised to introduce
ETP as an operational framework. Section~4.4 reports the G5 exploratory
results with appropriate hedging.

\msloc{Section~3.5 (ETP as framework, revised); Section~6 (prospective
calibration protocol added); Section~4.4 (G5 results).}

\rconcern{Reviewer \#1}{Concern \#2}{Separating confounds from pressure effects}

\rquote{How are quantization, optimizer state, data order, and model-specific
dynamics separated from the claimed pressure effects?}

\arhead{Author response:}
Each potential confound is addressed through a dedicated control, now
documented in Section~3.9 (``Sources of variation and experimental controls'')
with Table~2 (tab:controls).

For quantization: all QLoRA experiments use 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4) with
adapters in bf16. Ablation G3 reruns the same protocol with standard bf16
precision and no base-weight quantization, three seeds at each of $r=16$ and
$r=256$. At $r=16$, bf16 achieves Gen10\,=\,97.4\% (76/78 per seed), while
the original NF4 single-seed result was 96.2\% (75/78); these use different
seeds and pipelines and are not a paired comparison. At $r=256$, bf16
achieves Gen10\,=\,79.1\%, within 0.0\,pp of the NF4 G1$+$G4 mean under
the same seeds and matched protocol. Quantization is therefore not the
primary driver of the regime separation.

For optimizer state: a fresh optimizer is instantiated at every generation;
no momentum or adaptive-rate state is carried forward. This is held constant
across all conditions within a backbone.

For data order: ablation G4 runs Qwen at $r=256$ with three independent
shuffle seeds (42/101/202), all other conditions identical to G1
(seeds~15/137/256). A permutation test comparing Gen10 between the two groups
gives $p=0.70$ (two-sided, $N=3+3$). Because seed and data order varied
simultaneously in G4, this result does not isolate order alone; it establishes
that the combined variance is indistinguishable from seed-to-seed variance in
G1. The combined $N=6$ set yields Gen10\,=\,$79.7\%\pm1.7$\,pp.

Following the reviewer's implicit distinction, we now separate ETP variables
into two classes. \emph{ETP-design} variables are known before training:
nominal rank, learning rate, training steps, and synthetic exposure fraction.
\emph{ETP-observed} variables are measured after training: effective rank,
mean absolute weight drift $d(t)$, response length, and SDI-3. The latter
serve as post-hoc diagnostic proxies, not pre-specified predictors.

For the central Qwen result, the $r=16$ condition uses three G3/bf16 runs
(Gen10\,=\,76/78 per seed) and $r=256$ uses six G1$+$G4 runs (mean
79.7\%$\pm$1.7\,pp). The 17.7\,pp arithmetic gap is a descriptive
observation, not a confirmatory permutation test, because the two groups
differ simultaneously in rank, quantization, and data-order protocol. The
cleanest rank-only comparison is the G3 paired design: same seeds, same bf16,
rank the only variable; per-seed Gen10 differences are $+15$, $+13$, $+15$
items out of 78 (mean $+18.4$\,pp); sign-flip $p=0.25$ ($N=3$).

For Gemma~3, the $r=4$ vs.\ $r=16$ contrast ($N=5+5$, $|K_0|=46$) gives
$\Delta=+37.8$\,pp; bilateral permutation over $\binom{10}{5}=252$ allocations
gives $p=0.0079$ (independent seeds) or $p=0.0625$ (paired sign-flip).

\arhead{Author action:}
We added Section~3.9 and Table~2 documenting all controls. The ETP-design vs.\
ETP-observed distinction was introduced explicitly in Section~3.5 and Table~1.
The Qwen comparison now reports the descriptive 17.7\,pp gap and the G3
paired result ($p=0.25$, $N=3$) separately. Gemma~3 reports both permutation
tests with the seed-assignment caveat.

\rconcern{Reviewer \#1}{Concern \#3}{External validity of the evaluation}

\rquote{Do the conclusions hold for longer answers, multi-hop questions, other
domains, and larger factual evaluation sets?}

\arhead{Author response:}
Three distinct concerns are raised: the evaluation metric, the dataset scope,
and the extension to other domains.

The primary metric uses a bidirectional substring criterion, intentionally
permissive to handle short-answer variation and aliasing. Configurations that
generate longer responses may receive inflated retention scores. TriviaQA was
chosen to isolate factual retention from reasoning or stylistic confounds;
short answers minimise format-dependent evaluation noise and the substring
criterion is resilient to verbosity changes. The limitation is that findings
may not transfer to long-form generation, multi-hop reasoning, open-ended
dialogue, or code synthesis; this scope restriction is stated explicitly in
Section~6.

The held-out evaluation set contains 200 questions, of which $|K_0|$ are
answered correctly at generation~0 (e.g., $|K_0|=78$ for Qwen). This set is
disjoint from the 2,000-question synthetic training stream. Scaling the
evaluation set would alter the training-data composition and is deferred to
future work.

\arhead{Author action:}
We performed a strict exact-match re-evaluation on saved predictions for all
reported conditions. Under the strict scorer, absolute retention values are
lower, but the direction of every regime classification is unchanged. This
re-evaluation is reported in supplementary Table~S2 and discussed in
Section~6. The scope restriction to factoid single-answer evaluation is stated
explicitly in Section~6.

\msloc{Section~6, ll.~2094--2100 and ll.~2181--2193; supplementary
Table~S2; Section~3.1 (evaluation protocol).}

\rconcern{Reviewer \#1}{Concern \#4}{Exposure reduction: correction, pre-registered result, and mechanism}

\rquote{Why does the five-percent exposure reduction stabilize Qwen but not
Gemma~3, and does this generalize across ranks and seeds?}

\arhead{Author response:}
This question led directly to identifying a pipeline inconsistency in the
original submission and to a substantially revised, pre-registered experiment.

The original submission reported that a ${\sim}5\%$ reduction of synthetic
training examples raised retention by $+9.0$ and $+9.4$\,pp for conditions
C3 and C5 respectively. We subsequently identified that C1 (the baseline) and
C5 (the intervention) had been produced by different evaluation pipelines,
differing in the $K_0$ construction procedure and the data-loading code path.
When C1 and C5 are re-run under an identical pipeline with paired seeds, the
effect of ${\sim}5\%$ example removal is only $+1.9$\,pp, not distinguishable
from zero at $n=3$. This error was uncovered as a direct result of the
reviewer's question.

The pre-registered dose--response experiment (Bloco~7, protocol committed as
\texttt{a916ebf} before any run) varies synthetic exposure at 0\%, 10\%, 25\%,
and 50\% removal across five paired seeds on Qwen~2.5~1.5B at $r=256$.
At Gen~5, gains are uniform across doses: $+3.6$\,pp (10\%, $p=0.009$),
$+3.8$\,pp (25\%, $p=0.003$), $+4.4$\,pp (50\%, $p<0.001$). At Gen~10,
the benefit is dose-graded: $+1.8$\,pp at 10\% ($p=0.160$, not significant),
$+4.9$\,pp at 25\% ($p=0.005$, 95\,\% CI $[2.5, 7.2]$), and
$+9.2$\,pp at 50\% ($p<0.001$, 95\,\% CI $[6.9, 11.6]$). At 50\% removal
the Gen~5$\to$10 slope is $\BsevenSlopeFifty$\,pp/generation versus
${-1.13}$\,pp/generation at 0\%, strongly attenuating progressive
loss and shifting the system toward the Bounded regime.

The pre-registered prediction that 50\% removal would match halving the
learning rate from $\eta=10^{-5}$ to $5\times10^{-6}$ yielded a similar
point estimate (Gen~5 retention 89.7\% vs.\ 90.2\%; Gen~10 difference
$+0.4$\,pp, 90\,\% CI $[-0.8, 1.6]$). Formal equivalence was not
established.

A parallel pre-registered dose--response on Gemma~3~1B~IT at $r=10$ (G2,
three paired seeds, $|K_{0,\mathrm{G2}}|=44$) shows that 50\% removal
raises Gen~10 retention by $\GtwoDeltatenFifty$\,pp
($p=\GtwoPtenFifty$, 95\,\% CI $[\GtwoCItenLoFifty, \GtwoCItenHiFifty]$).
However, the Gen~5$\to$10 slope in the G2 50\% arm ($-0.91$\,pp/generation)
equals that of the 0\% arm ($-0.91$\,pp/generation) within rounding: the
exposure effect on Gemma~3 manifested as a Gen~10 retention gain without
reducing the rate of progressive loss. The mechanism was not established; a
steps-matched control was inconclusive at $n=3$.

\arhead{Author action:}
We acknowledged the pipeline inconsistency explicitly in the revised
manuscript and archived the original C2--C5 results in the replication
repository for transparency. Section~4.5 was rewritten to present the
pre-registered B7 dose--response results for both Qwen (five seeds per dose)
and Gemma~3 G2 (three seeds, 50\% removal), with paired $t$-tests and 95\,\%
confidence intervals. The learning-rate equivalence prediction is reported
with its 90\,\% CI and without claiming formal equivalence.

\msloc{Section~4.5 (B7 and G2 dose--response; pre-registration; LR
equivalence); Section~6 (mechanism limitation); replication repository
(original C1--C5 archived).}

Dear Editor and Reviewers,

We thank you for your careful and constructive evaluation of our manuscript.
Your comments prompted substantive revisions to the experimental account,
statistical reporting, notation, and scope of our conclusions. We respond to
each comment below and indicate the corresponding changes in the manuscript.

The most consequential correction concerns the original synthetic-exposure
comparison. During revision, we identified that its baseline and intervention
conditions had been evaluated through different pipeline paths, including
differences in the construction of the baseline-correct question set
($K_0$). Consequently, we no longer rely on that comparison as evidence for
the previously reported intervention effect. The revised manuscript instead
presents paired dose--response experiments conducted under consistent
pipelines. On Qwen, reducing synthetic exposure attenuated the
Generation~5--10 retention decline at the 50\% reduction level. On Gemma~3,
the corresponding intervention improved Generation~10 retention but did not
attenuate the decline over that interval. We have revised the interpretation
to preserve this distinction rather than proposing a common mechanism that
the experiments do not establish.

We have also clarified the status of effective training pressure (ETP). It is
now presented as an operational framework for organizing experimental
conditions and their observed diagnostics, not as a validated universal
scalar predictor or an architecture-independent threshold. Additional
revisions distinguish controlled comparisons from descriptive contrasts,
report the available seed-level uncertainty, define the notation used in the
equations and figures, and state the limits of the factual-retention
evaluation.

The responses that follow identify the action taken for each concern and
direct the reader to the relevant part of the revised manuscript. Revised
manuscript passages quoted in this letter appear in
\textcolor{chgcolor}{blue}.

We sincerely thank Reviewer~\#1 for the thorough and balanced assessment of this work. The four reservations identified in the general evaluation directly shaped the priorities of the revision, and we are grateful for the level of engagement with the experimental design. Addressing these reservations has substantially improved the precision of the claims and the transparency of the limitations.

We accept all four reservations in full and have restructured the
manuscript so that each is stated explicitly as a property of the evidence.

\textbf{Reservation~1: ETP is not yet a transferable quantitative predictor.}
ETP is now described as an operational experimental framework, not a
validated predictor. \newtext{The ETP index $\Pi$ is an exploratory
post-training summary; it is not available prospectively and has not been
validated as a transferable quantitative predictor across architectures or
datasets.} This sentence appears in Section~3.5, the Abstract, and~\S7.

\textbf{Reservation~2: The main metric uses one short-answer dataset.}
A strict exact-match sensitivity analysis was investigated and is reported
here as a methodological finding rather than a confirmatory table.
$K_0$ was defined by the bidirectional substring criterion; under strict
exact-match the generation-0 baseline performance is very low, because
TriviaQA aliases rarely form exact substrings of the generated output. More
critically, strict EM scores vary with response length in the opposite
direction to retention: as training progresses, configurations that produce
shorter outputs show higher EM while those that produce longer outputs show
lower EM, independently of factual content. Strict EM therefore measures
format convergence rather than factual retention, making it unsuitable as
a cross-condition sensitivity check. The scoring leniency concern is stated
as a genuine limitation in Section~7: for configurations that produce verbose
responses (notably $r=128$), the reported retention may partially reflect
format effects. This is now documented rather than controlled for, and the
evaluation scope is bounded explicitly: \newtext{The evaluation is conducted
on short-answer factual questions (TriviaQA); results for longer answers,
multi-hop questions, or other domains constitute an open empirical question.}

\textbf{Reservation~3: Several analyses are single-seed.}
The seed policy is now explicit at the level of individual claims
(Section~3.8). Headline conditions use three to six independent seeds;
intermediate ablations use one seed and are labeled descriptive; the two
categories are never pooled.

\textbf{Reservation~4: The exposure intervention is shown for mainly one Qwen condition.}
The single-seed C1/C5 pilot has been replaced by two pre-registered
dose--response experiments (B7: Qwen, five seeds; G2: Gemma~3, three seeds),
each at four dose levels within a single consistent evaluation pipeline.

\rconcern{Reviewer \#1, Concern \#1}{Prospective calibration of ETP}{How can effective training pressure be quantitatively calibrated and prospectively tested on an unseen backbone or dataset?}

\arhead{Author response:}
The revision corrects this at every level of the manuscript.

\textbf{What the original manuscript implied and why it is incorrect.}
The original manuscript introduced the ETP index $\Pi$ as a unified
quantitative measure that combines adapter rank, learning rate, and
effective rank into a single scalar. While this framing is useful for
organizing the experimental observations post hoc, it implies that $\Pi$
could be computed before training (using only the nominal design parameters)
and used to predict where a new configuration will fall in the regime
structure. This implication is incorrect. The effective rank component of
$\Pi$ is measured after training; it is not available prospectively.
Furthermore, the attempted cross-architecture normalizations showed that
the observed regime boundaries do not align across Qwen~2.5~1.5B and
Gemma~3~1B: the boundary falls between effective ranks ${}\sim3$--$6$
on Gemma~3 and between $50$--$88$ on Qwen. A single scalar therefore
cannot predict regime membership across backbones without a backbone-specific
calibration step.

\textbf{What the revised manuscript says instead.}
The revised manuscript distinguishes two classes of variable explicitly in
Section~3.5. ETP-design variables are nominal adapter rank, learning rate,
planned training steps, and synthetic data dose; these are fixed before
training and directly manipulated in the factorial design.
ETP-observed variables are effective rank and mean absolute weight drift
$d(t)$; these are measured after training as diagnostic proxies for how
much pressure was actually applied. The text now states:
\newtext{The ETP index $\Pi$ is an exploratory post-training summary
of the variables manipulated in this study. It is not available
prospectively and has not been validated as a transferable quantitative
predictor across architectures or datasets.} This sentence appears in
Section~3.5, is restated in the abstract, and is reflected in the
conclusion.

\textbf{What a genuine prospective test would require.}
A prospective calibration on an unseen backbone would require: (i)~a
pilot sweep over two nominal ranks spanning roughly one order of magnitude,
conducted for five recursive generations with a single seed, recording
effective rank per generation; (ii)~a backbone-local calibration step
estimating the regime boundary as the interval between the highest pilot
configuration whose Gen~1--5 retention slope is indistinguishable from
zero and the lowest whose slope is negative; (iii)~pre-specification of
two configurations on opposite sides of the calibrated interval, together
with the outcome criterion and decision rule, before any further run;
(iv)~evaluation of both configurations to Gen~10 with at least three
seeds; and (v)~reporting of the pre-registered prediction, the observed
classification, and any discrepancy, irrespective of direction. This
protocol is added to Section~6 as a concrete target for future work,
not as an experiment already completed.

\textbf{The G5 experiment and its scope.}
The exploratory G5 experiment provides a narrower check: Gen~5 retention
was ordered in the predicted direction across all three single-seed cells
($96.2\,\%$, $89.7\,\%$, $85.9\,\%$), but one of the three pre-specified
regime classifications was not met: the high-pressure cell was predicted
Degradative and returned Bounded. No threshold was pre-specified for G5,
and it did not evaluate an unseen backbone or dataset. The revised
manuscript characterizes G5 as exploratory evidence for the ordering
of regimes, not as a confirmatory prospective test.

\rconcern{Reviewer \#1, Concern \#3}{External validity of the evaluation}{Do the conclusions hold for longer answers, multi-hop questions, other domains, and larger factual evaluation sets?}

\arhead{Author response:}

\textbf{Scope of the evaluation dataset.}
The evaluation uses a held-out set of 200 TriviaQA questions, disjoint
from the 2,000-question synthetic training stream. Retention is measured
as the fraction of questions in $K_0$ (those answered correctly at
generation~0) that remain correctly answered at generation~$t$. For Qwen
under the reported protocol, $|K_0|=78$. This is a small held-out set by
the standards of NLP evaluation. Larger factual test sets would improve
the assessment of sampling variability and provide more stable retention

composition of the synthetic training stream and would not address
generalization to other domains or answer formats. The revised manuscript

investigated; the finding is reported here because it is informative about

was defined by the bidirectional criterion. Under strict exact-match,
the generation-0 baseline performance is very low on TriviaQA aliases

\rsub{(a)}{No formal tests, confidence intervals, or $p$-values.}
Inferential statistics have been added wherever the design admits them,
and the design is now stated alongside each test rather than left to be
inferred. For the Gemma~3 anchor-rank contrast ($N=5$ per rank,
$|K_0|=46$), the mean difference is $+37.8$\,pp; because the
archived data do not confirm whether seeds were paired across ranks, we
report both possibilities --- an exact sign-flip test at $p=0.0625$ if
paired, a bilateral permutation test over $\binom{10}{5}=252$ allocations
at $p=0.0079$ if independent --- with Hedges' $g \approx 13.3$ under
either reading. For the Qwen rank contrast, the clean comparison is the
G3 paired design (same seeds, same precision, rank the only variable):
per-seed differences of $+15$, $+13$ and $+15$ items out of 78, mean
$+18.4$\,pp, exact sign test $p=0.25$ at $N=3$. We report that $p$-value
rather than suppress it: the direction is consistent across all three
pairs, but three pairs cannot produce a small $p$, and we say so. For the
two dose--response experiments, paired $t$-tests with 95\,\% confidence
intervals are reported at every dose level. Where groups are not
exchangeable under the relevant null --- the 17.7\,pp gap between the
three-seed $r=16$ condition and the six-seed $r=256$ estimate, which
differ in rank, precision protocol and seed assignment simultaneously ---
the comparison is labeled descriptive and no test is reported.
\newtext{(\S4.1; \S4.2.1; \S4.5; \S4.6)}

\rsub{(b)}{The framework is not quantified.}
Correct, and the revision no longer implies otherwise. No scalar
predictor is derived. The manuscript now separates the variables fixed
before training --- nominal rank, learning rate, training steps, dose
fraction --- from the quantities measured after it --- effective rank,
weight drift, response length, SDI-3 --- and states that the latter
\newtext{are not available prospectively; they therefore serve as

operational index $\Pi = \mathrm{erank}(\Delta W)\cdot(\eta/10^{-5})$ is

\newtext{not used as a validated quantitative threshold}. Developing a
quantitative formulation is listed as future work, with the protocol a
prospective test would require specified in Section~6.
\newtext{(\S3.5; \S6)}

\rsub{(c)}{Normalisation failed with no alternative proposed.}
The reviewer asks for the constructive half of a negative result, and the
original manuscript supplied only the negative half. Five normalizations
were tested --- by hidden dimension, its square root, layer count,
attention heads, and key--value heads --- and none aligned the thresholds
across backbones (Table~13). We retain that table as a reported
negative result and now propose what we believe the finding implies: that
a transferable constant should not be sought, and that calibration must
be per-backbone. Section~6 specifies the procedure concretely --- a pilot
sweep of two ranks spanning roughly an order of magnitude, five
generations, single seed; the boundary estimated as the interval between
the highest configuration whose early retention slope is indistinguishable
from zero and the lowest whose slope is negative --- together with its
cost, which is approximately two short runs per backbone. We present this
as the practical alternative to a universal normalization rather than as a
validated method, since we have not executed it on an unseen backbone.
\newtext{(\S4.2.3; \S6; Table~13)}
